# Media map — Sullivan County, New Hampshire

Written by `district-media-map`. A working contact map, not a list of names.

This is a **golden reference output** and the news-desert case. It profiles no candidate. Every
editorial contact below is copied verbatim from the outlet's own published contact or submission
page, with the URL and date recorded. **Nothing here is a guessed address pattern.**

## Assessment

Sullivan County is coded as a **zero-source county** by Northwestern University's 2025 State of
Local News Report — one of only two counties in New Hampshire, with Cheshire, "where residents
have fewer than three local papers based within them," and the only one with none ([Keene
Sentinel, "Twin State Valley reflects on life without local news," by Abigail
Ham](https://www.keenesentinel.com/news/local/twin-state-valley-nh-vt-reflects-on-life-without-local-news/article_a516c6ec-1e12-417b-a511-f71958d9d06d.html),
retrieved 2026-08-04). The *Eagle Times*, which had covered Claremont and Newport under various
names for 192 years, went dark in July 2025; `eagletimes.com` is now a parked hosting page
(checked 2026-08-04). The Newport weekly *Argus-Champion* closed in 2008 after 185 years.

**But this scan does not classify Sullivan as a desert, and the reason is a finding worth
carrying back into the skill.** Two things are actually happening in the county that a
base-location county count cannot see:

1. **The Vermont Journal & The Shopper**, published out of Ludlow, Vermont, launched a third
   title — **The Message of the Week** — expressly "to serve Claremont and local New Hampshire
   towns via drop locations," and it covers **Claremont City Council meeting by meeting**
   ([Journal LLC comes to
   Claremont](https://vermontjournal.com/featured-articles/journal-llc-comes-to-claremont/),
   retrieved 2026-08-04). Because it is based in Vermont, it does not register in a
   county-of-publication tally of Sullivan County.
2. **Claremont Community Media Center (CCTV)**, a 501(c)(3) PEG access operation, records and
   streams City Council, Planning Board, Zoning Board, Conservation Commission, the School Board
   *and* the Sullivan County Board of Commissioners. Its video-on-demand catalogue held **6,676
   shows** at the time of this scan ([CCTV
   API](https://reflect-claremont.cablecast.tv/cablecastapi/v1/shows), retrieved 2026-08-04). It
   is not a newsroom and produces no written reporting, so it registers nowhere as "local news"
   — but the county's public record of local government is substantially intact because of it.
3. **A machine is filling part of the gap.** [MeetingWatch](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/),
   an automated transcription-and-analysis service, has published reports on **38 Claremont
   meetings across 5 boards since February 2026**, and covers four more Sullivan County towns —
   Newport, Plainfield, Springfield and Sunapee (retrieved 2026-08-04). It attends nothing and
   employs no reporter; it reads the video CCTV posts. It is a useful index and **not a citable
   source** — its own footer says the analysis "may contain errors," and its member index
   misspells half the council. Full entry and evidence below.

Against the skill's own behavioral thresholds this comes out **thin, not desert** — one outlet
produces original reporting on a governing body here, at roughly monthly-or-better cadence.
**With one large exception: the Sullivan County government itself is a desert.** I found no
coverage anywhere of the July 2, 2026 Delegation convention that cut the TRAILS program 7–5 and
raised county property taxes 6.45%, and none of the Board of Commissioners' July 20 follow-up.
The city of Claremont is thinly covered; the county that taxes all fifteen towns is not covered
at all.

What a campaign can realistically earn here: a Vermont Journal meeting story if you appear at the
meeting, a Valley News story if the subject is big enough to travel, and essentially nothing else
without paying for it. **Note in particular that The Vermont Journal charges for political
letters** — see its entry below. In this county, owned and borrowed surfaces have to do most of
the work.

### MeetingWatch — a machine, not a newsroom

Listed because it is now the largest secondary index of Claremont's public record, and because a
campaign will find it and needs to know exactly what it is before quoting it.

- **Type:** automated meeting transcription and analysis service. Self-described as "a civic
  accountability project." **Not journalism, and it does not claim to be.**
- **Owner:** **UNVERIFIED.** The site names no publisher, editor, funder or responsible person on
  its About page. It solicits donations ("Covering Claremont costs real money") and runs
  [@MeetingWatchNH on X](https://twitter.com/MeetingWatchNH). **Not knowing who operates it is a
  reason for caution, not a reason to ignore it.**
- **Coverage area:** 54+ communities nationally. In Sullivan County it covers **five towns —
  Claremont, Newport, Plainfield, Springfield and Sunapee** ([MeetingWatch community
  list](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/city-council/), retrieved 2026-08-04). In Claremont
  it tracks 5 boards, 38 meetings analyzed, 196 decisions logged and 163 public comments, with
  history since February 2026 ([MeetingWatch,
  Claremont](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/), retrieved 2026-08-04). For the City Council
  specifically: 13 meetings tracked, latest July 8 ([MeetingWatch, Claremont City
  Council](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/city-council/), retrieved 2026-08-04).
- **Original reporting?** No. Nobody attends. The published method is: collect the video, agenda
  and minutes automatically from the town's own site and streaming platform; transcribe
  word-for-word with speakers labeled; run a fixed question set over the full transcript; publish
  within about a day ([MeetingWatch, About](https://meetingwatch.org/about/), retrieved
  2026-08-04). Output includes a summary, a decision count, quoted statements, and timestamps that
  jump into the embedded CCTV video.
- **What it is genuinely good for.** It is a *finding aid*. For the June 10, 2026 council meeting
  it records a 3.9-hour session, 91 speakers, 6 public comments and 22 decisions, and points at
  the minute mark of each exchange ([MeetingWatch,
  2026-06-10](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/city-council/2026-06-10/), retrieved
  2026-08-04). Using it to locate the two minutes of video you need, then watching those two
  minutes, is a legitimate and very efficient research method.
- **What it must not be used for, with the evidence.** Its own footer says: "Analysis is
  AI-generated and may contain errors. Always verify with source records," and each report repeats
  "always verify against the actual meeting before relying on it" (retrieved 2026-08-04). That
  warning is warranted, and it is checkable from the outside:
  - Its council member index lists both `gerard` and `girard` as separate members. Claremont has
    one mayor, **Dale Girard** ([City of Claremont, City
    Council](https://www.claremontnh.com/council-minutes), retrieved 2026-08-04).
  - It renders Councilor **Koloski** as "Kowalski" and Assistant Mayor **Matteau** as "Mattel" —
    both misspellings drawn from transcript speaker labels, both wrong against the city's own
    roster (same sources, retrieved 2026-08-04).
  - Quotes are attributed to constructions like "Speaker S31 (Councilor Kowalski)," meaning the
    speaker was inferred rather than identified.

  **So: a campaign may use it to find where in the video something happened. A campaign may not
  quote it, cite it as a source of fact, or attribute a statement to a named official on its
  authority.** Doing so would mean republishing an unreviewed machine assertion about a real
  person under the campaign's own name — the precise failure this repository exists to prevent.
  See [`reference/shared-rules.md`](../../reference/shared-rules.md).
- **Independent corroboration, in its favor.** Its account of the June 10 water-rate vote — "split
  5-4," council choosing the "minimum" increase model — matches the tally recovered by OCR from
  the scanned council minutes bound into the June 24 packet. Two unrelated machine derivations
  agreeing is meaningful. It is still not the signed record.
- **Citation value as an earned-media target:** **zero.** There is no reporter, no letters page,
  no pitch address. It has a feedback form and invites people to submit a link to a meeting video
  it is missing, which is the one useful thing you can send it.
- **Paywall:** none. Free to read.
- **Source:** https://meetingwatch.org/about/ and https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/, both
  checked 2026-08-04.

- **Citation value:** high (8/10) — no paywall, dated stable URLs, republication-friendly, and

- **Letters to the editor:** None. The Bulletin does not run a letters section.

- **Election policy:** implicit in the commentary rule above; no separate cutoff published.
- **Source:** https://newhampshirebulletin.com/about/ and
  https://newhampshirebulletin.com/republishing-guidelines/, checked 2026-08-04. The domain

## News desert

- **What is actually left, inside the county:** one weekly's Claremont edition (**The Message of
  the Week**, published from Ludlow, Vermont), **CCTV**, which is a video archive rather than a
  newsroom, and **MeetingWatch**, which is software. That is the entire apparatus. There is **no
  newspaper based in Sullivan County**, and there has not been since July 2025. Exactly one
  human being — Patrick O'Grady, part-time, for a paper 25 miles away in another state's media
  market — is paid to cover this county.
- **What none of them covers: the county government.** MeetingWatch tracks five Claremont boards
  — City Council, School Board, Planning, Zoning and Conservation — and no county body at all
  ([MeetingWatch, Claremont](https://meetingwatch.org/nh/claremont/), retrieved 2026-08-04). CCTV
  records the Board of Commissioners but publishes no account of it. No outlet covered the
  July 2 Delegation convention. **The machine, the video archive and the press have converged on
  the same blind spot**, and it happens to be the body that sets the county tax rate.
- **Nearest outlet that would plausibly cover a race here:** **The Valley News**, 25 miles north
  in West Lebanon. It already lists every significant Sullivan County town in its coverage area
  and employs the only reporter dedicated to the county — Patrick O'Grady, part-time. What would
  make them cover more: editor Matt Clary said the paper "would like to do more in Sullivan
  County, but that, despite being a large market, the area has acute market challenges for any
  news business," adding that "the people who need it the most don't get it" ([Keene
  Sentinel](https://www.keenesentinel.com/news/local/twin-state-valley-nh-vt-reflects-on-life-without-local-news/article_a516c6ec-1e12-417b-a511-f71958d9d06d.html),
  retrieved 2026-08-04). Practically: bring them a documented story they do not have to source
  themselves. The July 2 TRAILS roll call is exactly that, and it is sitting in a SharePoint
  folder nobody has opened.
- **Regional outlets that cover this county's issues occasionally:** the **Keene Sentinel**
  (Charlestown, cursorily; and it produced the definitive piece on the county's news desert), the
  **Concord Monitor**, and across the river the **Springfield Reporter**, which covers Springfield,
  Vermont only.
- **Statewide outlets:** **WMUR**, **NH Public Radio**, the **Union Leader** — all three have
  covered Claremont's fiscal problems, and all three left afterward.
- **Nonprofit statewide newsrooms — the underused channel:** New Hampshire has **two**, which is
  unusual and lucky. **New Hampshire Bulletin** (States Newsroom affiliate, no paywall,
  CC-licensed, already deep in the Claremont school story) and **InDepthNH.org** (The New
  Hampshire Center for Public Interest Journalism, no paywall, statehouse-focused, runs outside
  columnists). **Read the Bulletin's rule before pitching: it does not publish commentary from
  elected officials or candidates.** InDepthNH's rules are unverified and one email away. Both
  take news tips from anyone, and a documented county roll call is a news tip, not a pitch.
- **What replaces earned media here:**
  1. **The recorded public meeting.** CCTV records and archives everything. This is the county's
     one genuinely reliable, genuinely free public surface, and no other county in this exercise
     has anything comparable.
  2. **The county's own SharePoint record.** Draft minutes, the EFC budget presentation, the
     cross-county tax comparison — all public, all unread, all citable with a date and a URL.
     Whoever quotes them first is the only source on the subject.
  3. **MeetingWatch as a research shortcut, never as a citation.** It will tell you which minute
     of which video to watch. Watch it, then cite the video and the minutes. If a campaign ever
     finds itself repeating a claim because MeetingWatch said it, stop.
  4. **Paid political letters in The Message**, at $75 per 200 words. Unpleasant, but it is a
     known price for known reach in the one paper that lands in Claremont.
  5. **Ballotpedia, VOTE411, the county party page, and the campaign's own answer pages**, which
     carry disproportionate weight precisely because nothing else here does.
  6. **Doors and the senior center.** Not a media channel, but in a county where the community
     calendar in a free weekly is the most complete published record of where people gather, it
     belongs on the list.

**A note on the count.** `outlets_found: 10` is generous and should be read with the entries, not
on its own. Of the ten, **one is based in Sullivan County and it is a public-access TV station**;
one is software; one is a Vermont weekly; one is a regional daily with a part-time stringer here;
and the remaining six are statewide or out-of-county outlets that visit for a crisis. A single
number cannot carry that, which is an argument for reading the map rather than the frontmatter.

**A thin media map is not a failed scan.** The finding that should change the plan here is
narrower than "no press": Claremont city government is thinly but genuinely covered, while
**Sullivan County government — which cut a treatment program 7–5 and raised property taxes 6.45%
five weeks ago — has no press at all.** A campaign that plans around getting covered will be
disappointed. A campaign that plans around being the only person who has read the record, and
publishing that on a surface it controls, is playing to the actual conditions.
