# Media map — Whatcom County, Washington

Written by `district-media-map`. A working contact map, not a list of names. Every row should
be actionable today: who to email, at what address, about what, by when, under what rules.

Ranked by **relevance and citation value**, not circulation.

This is a **golden reference output**: a real run of the skill against a real place. It profiles
no candidate. Reporter and editor contacts below are copied verbatim from the outlets' own
published submission and contact pages, with the URL and date recorded on each. **Nothing here
is a guessed address pattern.**

## Assessment

Whatcom County is one of the healthier local-news markets of its size in the country, and the
scan supports that rather than assuming it. Two independent newsrooms cover county government
directly: **Cascadia Daily News**, a for-profit daily-digital/weekly-print outlet founded in 2022
with a newsroom of roughly sixteen, which published four original stories tracking the ferry
levy from introduction to adoption; and **Salish Current**, a nonprofit newsroom covering
Whatcom, Skagit and San Juan counties that was publishing several times a week during the scan
window. The **Bellingham Herald** (McClatchy) still covers the county council, though its August
2 ferry-levy follow-up carried the byline "Edited by Scot Heisel with AI assistance," which is
worth knowing before treating it as an independent confirmation of anything. Below those,
**Whatcom Watch** runs a monthly item-by-item ledger of county council and port commission votes
— genuinely unusual and genuinely useful — at roughly a two-month lag. The **Lynden Tribune** and
**Ferndale Record** (same publisher) cover the north-county cities that the Bellingham-centric
outlets skip, and **The Northern Light** covers Blaine and Point Roberts. A campaign here can
realistically earn coverage: there are working reporters, published submission rules, and clear
word limits. The honest constraint is competition, not scarcity — the ferry-levy story was
covered well enough by CDN before the vote that there is nothing left to add, while the
$225-million jail scope fight and the Bellingham rent-software initiative have real, documented
holes in them.

**Election-cutoff status: none confirmed.** I found published letter and op-ed rules for CDN and
Salish Current but **no published pre-election cutoff at any outlet in this county.** Treat every
"Election policy" line below as unverified and call before October.

## News desert

**Whatcom County is not a news desert.** By the skill's own thresholds — *healthy:* two or more
outlets produce original reporting on this district's governing bodies most weeks — it clears the
bar with room to spare. Cascadia Daily News and Salish Current both do original reporting on
county government; the Bellingham Herald and Whatcom Watch add a third and fourth layer; the
Lynden Tribune, Ferndale Record and The Northern Light cover the smaller cities.

This section is filled in anyway, briefly, because two things in the scan complicate the happy
picture and a campaign should know both.

- **What is actually left:** eight outlets, of which four (CDN, Salish Current, the Herald,
  Whatcom Watch) demonstrably touch the county council. That is a strong market for a county of
  this size.
- **Where the coverage is thinnest:** the **special districts**. Fire Protection District No. 1
  put two levy lid lifts on the 2026 ballot and I could not locate its agenda portal, its minutes,
  or any coverage of the board meetings that adopted them. Glacier Fire & Rescue and Fire
  Protection District 21 are in the same position. Every outlet in this county covers the county
  council; **none of them appears to cover the fire districts**, which are the bodies actually
  raising property taxes on the November ballot. That is a real, narrow desert inside a healthy
  market, and it is precisely where the skill's "boring bodies, real money" guidance points.
- **Statewide nonprofit newsroom:** **Washington State Standard**
  (washingtonstatestandard.com), part of the States Newsroom network. Free, no paywall,
  republication-friendly, and consistently underused by down-ballot campaigns. Its natural beat
  here is the Legislative District 40 and 42 races rather than a fire levy.
- **Public radio:** **KUOW** (kuow.org, HTTP 200 confirmed 2026-08-04) covers Puget Sound and
  runs a statehouse operation; Whatcom is at the edge of its range but a state-legislative angle
  can travel.
- **What has to carry more weight regardless:** the **Local Voters' Pamphlet** and the **LWV
  candidate interviews**, both of which are free, both of which produce a durable public artifact,
  and neither of which depends on an editor's news judgment. Given that the ferry-levy story was
  already covered well by CDN before the vote, the marginal value of a pitch on that subject is
  near zero; the marginal value of being the only person who has published a readable explanation
  of the $225 million jail budget cap is high.

A thin media map is not a failed scan. Here the map is not thin — but the **UNVERIFIED count is
high**, and that is the honest headline. Seven of the eight outlets have at least one unverified
submission field, and **not one outlet in this county has a confirmed pre-election cutoff date.**
That is the field the skill warns you cannot afford to have wrong in October, and it is still
blank on August 4.
