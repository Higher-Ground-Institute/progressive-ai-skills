# Pitch — [outlet] / [reporter]

## Who this is going to and why

- **Reporter and beat:**
- **A recent story of theirs this connects to:** with URL and date — read it before pitching
- **Why this reporter specifically:** if the answer is "they cover this county," that is thin.
  If the answer is "they wrote about the rate increase in March and this is the next
  development," that is a pitch.
- **Submission rules that apply:** word limit, exclusivity, election-period restrictions
- **Source of those rules:** URL and date checked

## Subject line

Under 60 characters. Says what the story is, not that a candidate exists.

## The email

Written to a journalist, in the campaign's own voice, signed by a named human at the
campaign. **Never written as a journalist, never anonymous, never pretending to be an
unaffiliated tipster.**

Structure that works:

1. One sentence on what is happening and why it is news now
2. Two or three sentences of the specific evidence, with the primary record linked
3. What the campaign can provide: the candidate on the record, the underlying documents, a
   local resident willing to talk
4. A concrete, low-friction ask
5. Name, role, phone, email

Keep story tips under 200 words and op-ed/LTE cover emails, interview offers, and document
shares under 150. The submission body lives in the linked placement and follows the outlet's
limit.

## What is attached or linked

- Link to the published answer page or brief, or the approved op-ed/LTE placement
- Link to the primary records, not just the campaign's summary of them

## The honest self-check

- [ ] Is this actually news, or is it the campaign wanting coverage? If it is the second one,
      do not send it. A reporter remembers who wastes their time.
- [ ] Would this be a story if a different candidate's campaign sent it?
- [ ] Is every factual claim in the pitch sourced and correct?

## Follow-up

One follow-up, one week later, one paragraph. Then stop.
