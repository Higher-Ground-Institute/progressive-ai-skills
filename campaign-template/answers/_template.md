# [The question, as a headline a voter would recognize]

**The answer, in the first paragraph.** State the position in the opening sentence. Do not
open with background, do not open with a story, do not open with "as I've traveled across
this district." A voter who reads only the first three sentences should know exactly where
this candidate stands.

## Why

Two to four paragraphs of evidence. Specific numbers, specific dates, specific dollar
figures, specific votes, each one sourced inline:

> The county raised the stormwater fee from $4.50 to $11.25 a month in January
> ([Ordinance 2026-14](https://example.gov/ord-2026-14), adopted 2026-01-14).

Not "rates have gone up a lot." The number, the date, the record.

Use the strongest verified evidence available. Preserve qualifications and link the primary
record where possible.

## What I would do

The specific action, at the specific body, through the specific mechanism. "Fight for working
families" is not an action. "Vote against the franchise renewal unless it includes the
undergrounding schedule the 2024 study recommended" is.

## What I don't know yet

State the uncertainty. What would change the candidate's mind, what depends on information
they do not have, where reasonable people disagree.

Most candidates skip this section, which is exactly why including it is worth something. It
is also the honest thing to do.
