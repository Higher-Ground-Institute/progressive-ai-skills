# AI Answer-Engine Citation: Research Findings

**Compiled:** Aug 4, 2026
**Purpose:** Evidence base for an open-source agent skill helping first-time down-ballot 2026 candidates publish citable information.
**Ethical constraint:** Publish true information in legitimate places. No output gaming, no astroturfing.

**Labeling key used throughout:**
- **(a) VENDOR** — documented guidance published by the platform itself
- **(b) STUDY** — empirical research with stated sample sizes and numbers
- **(c) SPECULATION** — SEO-industry inference, not confirmed by vendor or controlled study
- **UNVERIFIED** — could not confirm in this session; do not ship without checking

---

## 0. The headline findings, up front

1. **Two things changed since most GEO advice was written.** Google's FAQ rich result is now *fully dead* — not just restricted, as it was in 2023 — as of May 7, 2026, and Google removed the documentation in June 2026. And Google has published an explicit "mythbusting" list telling site owners to *ignore* llms.txt, content chunking, and structured-data-for-AI. Both are (a) VENDOR.
2. **The single highest-stakes technical item for a campaign site is robots.txt.** A misconfigured file silently removes a candidate from ChatGPT search answers, Perplexity, and Claude's web search. Section 5 has the exact strings.
3. **The strongest measured correlates of AI visibility are off-site, not on-site** — being mentioned elsewhere on the web outcorrelates backlinks roughly 3:1 (b) STUDY. For a down-ballot candidate this means local press, county party pages, League of Women Voters guides, and Ballotpedia matter more than site markup. This is also the *ethical* path: earn real coverage.
4. **Q&A formatting alone does not help and may slightly hurt.** The best available controlled dataset found Q&A-formatted pages had ~5.7% *lower* answer influence than non-Q&A pages (b) STUDY. What helps is evidence density: definitions, numbers, comparisons, dates.

## 1. Google's official AI optimization guide

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide

**All of this section is (a) VENDOR.**

### 1.1 How the system works (Google's own framing)

Google states its generative features are "rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems" and names two mechanisms:

> "**Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG):** A technique (also known as grounding) used to improve the quality, accuracy, and freshness of AI responses by relying on our core Search ranking systems to retrieve relevant, up-to-date web pages from our Search index."

> "**Query fan-out:** A set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model to request more information and fetch additional relevant search results to address the user's query."

**Implication for the skill:** For Google surfaces specifically, there is no separate "AI channel." Ordinary indexing and ranking is the gate. A candidate page that cannot rank cannot be cited.

### 1.2 Crawlability and technical requirements — the hard eligibility gates

> "To be eligible to be shown in generative AI features on Google Search, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search **with a snippet**, fulfilling the Search technical requirements."

This is the most concrete, checkable rule in the entire document. Two failure modes it implies:
- `noindex` → ineligible
- `nosnippet` / `max-snippet:0` / `data-nosnippet` on the main content → ineligible even if indexed

> "In addition to the technical requirements for Search, a site must be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console to be eligible for display in generative AI features on Google Search."

> "**Follow crawling best practices.** To maximize your site's visibility in generative AI search features, ensure your content is crawlable, as Google Search generative AI models use publicly accessible, crawlable content to learn patterns and provide relevant, grounded responses."

On JavaScript — relevant because many campaign sites are built on SPA site builders:

> "Google is able to process content within JavaScript as long as it isn't blocked. That said, working on SEO with a website that uses JavaScript frameworks is generally more complex than when working with other kinds of websites."

On semantic HTML — note Google explicitly *downplays* this:

> "While it's not required to have perfectly semantic HTML (the web in general is not valid HTML, and Google can understand it), it's generally a good idea to try to use semantic HTML when possible, as it helps other types of users, such as screen readers, parse and navigate your web page more easily."

### 1.3 Content structure

> "**Organizing content in a way that helps your readers:** Write content for your human audience and make sure the content is well written and easy to follow. People generally appreciate it when web pages are organized by paragraphs and sections, along with **headings that provide a clear structure** to navigate content."

That is the *entirety* of Google's structural guidance. There is no answer-first recommendation, no word count, no heading-depth advice.

> "**Providing a unique point of view:** ... a first-hand review provides a unique perspective based on personal experience, whereas a summary of existing content simply restates information already available elsewhere."

**This maps unusually well onto a candidate skill.** A candidate's own position statements, biography, and voting rationale are inherently first-party, non-commodity content that exists nowhere else. That is exactly what Google says it wants — and it requires no manipulation.

Google explicitly warns against the tactic of spinning up a page per fan-out query:

> "While it might be tempting to create separate content for every possible variation of how people might search (for example, by focusing on other queries that people have asked, or fan-out queries), doing so primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses in Google Search **violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy**."

⚠️ **Skill design constraint:** any "generate a page for every question a voter might ask" feature risks tripping scaled content abuse. Keep page generation tied to genuine, distinct candidate positions.

### 1.4 Structured data — Google's actual position

> "**Overfocusing on structured data:** Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add. However, it's a good idea to continue using it as part of your overall SEO strategy, as it helps with being eligible for rich results on Google Search."

### 1.5 Google's mythbusting list (things to *not* do)

> "**LLMS.txt files and other 'special' markup:** You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search (including its generative AI capabilities), as **Google Search itself doesn't use them**."

> "**'Chunking' content:** There's no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI to better understand it. Google systems are able to understand the nuance of multiple topics on a page and show the relevant piece to users. However, sometimes shorter (or longer!) pages can work well depending on your audience and subject matter. **There's no ideal page length.**"

> "**Rewriting content just for AI systems:** You don't need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search. AI systems can understand synonyms and general meanings."

> "**Seeking inauthentic 'mentions':** ... seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem. Our core ranking systems focus on high-quality content while other systems block spam."

**The last one is worth quoting in the skill's README.** Google's own documentation independently endorses the project's ethical line: astroturfed mentions are both wrong and ineffective.

### 1.6 Measurement

> "To measure how your content is performing in generative AI features on Google Search and Discover, use the **Generative AI performance report in Search Console**."

This is the only free, first-party measurement surface available. Everything else on the market is third-party sampling.

## 2. Schema.org / JSON-LD for a candidate

### 2.1 What Google still supports — current gallery

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery — **(a) VENDOR**

Types relevant to a candidate that are **still supported**:

| Type | Google's description | Use for a candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Profile page** (`ProfilePage`) | "A page that primarily focuses on information about a single person or organization that is somehow affiliated with the overall website." | ✅ The correct wrapper for the candidate bio page |
| **Organization** | "Information about your organization, such as your logo, legal name, address, contact information... can show up in knowledge panels" | ✅ The campaign committee |
| **Article** / `NewsArticle` / `BlogPosting` | Article rich results | ✅ Press releases, position papers |
| **Q&A** (`QAPage`) | "web pages that contain data in a question and answer format, which is one question followed by its answers" | ⚠️ See 2.3 — probably not eligible |
| **Breadcrumb** | Site hierarchy | ✅ Cheap, harmless |

**`FAQPage` is absent from the gallery entirely.**

### 2.2 FAQPage — confirmed dead, and more dead than you thought

Your recollection was correct but understated. There were **two** events, confirmed from Google's official documentation changelog (**(a) VENDOR**, https://developers.google.com/search/updates):

**Event 1 — September 2023 (restriction):**
> "Updated the FAQ structured data documentation to state that the feature is only shown for **well-known, authoritative government and health websites**."

**Event 2 — May 2026 (full deprecation):**
> "**Deprecating the FAQ rich result feature.** What: Added a deprecation notice to the FAQ rich result documentation. Why: This feature will no longer appear in Google Search starting **May 7, 2026**."

**Event 3 — June 2026 (documentation removal):**
> "**Removing documentation for the FAQ rich result feature.** What: Removed documentation for the FAQ rich result feature. Why: The FAQ rich result feature is no longer shown in Google Search results."

**Net:** a down-ballot campaign site was never eligible under the 2023 rules (it is not a "well-known, authoritative government" site — a campaign is not a government), and as of May 7, 2026, *nobody* is. FAQPage produces zero visual rich results today.

### 2.3 ⚠️ QAPage is not the safe substitute people assume

`QAPage` remains in the gallery, and the SEO industry has widely recommended it as the FAQPage replacement. **(c) SPECULATION / caution:** Google's Q&A definition — "one question followed by **its answers**" (plural) — describes forum-style pages where users submit competing answers, not a campaign FAQ where the candidate is the sole voice.

**UNVERIFIED:** I did not fetch the QAPage content guidelines page in this session to confirm the exact eligibility wording. **Before the skill emits `QAPage` markup, verify against https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/qapage.** My strong prior is that a single-author campaign FAQ does not qualify and that emitting `QAPage` there is misrepresentative markup — which would also violate the project's ethical line, since it asserts something untrue about the page.

**Recommendation:** do not emit `QAPage` for candidate FAQs. Use ordinary headings and prose.

### 2.4 Is the markup still worth adding without rich results?

**Honest answer: yes, but for a different reason than people claim, and the effect is small.**

Arguments *for*, by evidence strength:

1. **(a) VENDOR — entity disambiguation, not citation.** `Organization` markup is documented to feed knowledge panels. For a candidate this is the genuinely valuable case: down-ballot candidates have common names and are constantly confused with namesakes. `sameAs` linking the candidate to their Ballotpedia entry, FEC/state filing page, and official social accounts is a truthful, machine-readable identity assertion. That is a real problem structured data actually solves.
2. **(a) VENDOR — Google says keep using it** ("it's a good idea to continue using it as part of your overall SEO strategy").
3. **(b) STUDY — measurably small.** The Discovered Labs analysis found a bundled "AEO checklist" including schema markup was "not a rounding error" but was dwarfed by other factors; AI-perceived domain authority had a mean absolute SHAP value of **0.38** versus **0.06** for the strongest page-level feature — roughly 6× (https://discoveredlabs.com/research/what-drives-ai-citations).

Arguments *against*:

4. **(a) VENDOR — Google explicitly says it is not required** for generative AI features and there is "no special schema.org markup you need to add."
5. **(c) SPECULATION — the "LLMs read your JSON-LD" claim is unsupported.** I found no vendor statement from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, or Perplexity that their retrieval pipelines parse JSON-LD as a distinct signal. **Mark any such claim UNVERIFIED.** The plausible mechanism is indirect: JSON-LD text is in the HTML, so it is in the fetched document, but that is not the same as it being weighted.

**Verdict for the skill:** emit `ProfilePage` + `Person` + `Organization` because it costs one template and it fixes a real identity-disambiguation problem for candidates. Do *not* market it to users as an AI-citation lever. Skip `FAQPage`. Skip `QAPage`.

### 2.5 `Article` — Google-supported properties

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article — **(a) VENDOR**

> "**There are no required properties**; instead, add the properties that apply to your content."

Recommended properties, verbatim scope:

| Property | Type | Google's note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `author` | Person or Organization | Follow author markup best practices |
| `author.name` | Text | Name **only** — no title, no honorific, no "posted by" |
| `author.url` | URL | "A link to a web page that uniquely identifies the author" |
| `datePublished` | DateTime | ISO 8601; "We recommend that you provide timezone information; otherwise, we will default to the timezone used by Googlebot" |
| `dateModified` | DateTime | ISO 8601, same timezone note |
| `headline` | Text | "Consider using a concise title, as long titles may be truncated" |
| `image` | ImageObject or URL | Min 50K pixels (w×h); recommend 16x9, 4x3, and 1x1 |

**Author markup rules that campaigns get wrong (all (a) VENDOR):**
- List multiple authors as separate array entries, never `"name": "Willow Lane, Regula Felix"`
- Use `Person` for people and `Organization` for organizations; "Don't use the `Thing` type"
- Put job title in `jobTitle`, not in `author.name`
- Publisher goes in `publisher`, not `author`

**Notes on correctness:**
- `@id` cross-references stitch `Person` ↔ `Organization` into one graph instead of two orphan blobs. This is the part most campaign sites get wrong.
- `sameAs` should contain **only URLs the campaign actually controls or that genuinely describe the candidate.** Listing a Wikipedia article that does not exist, or one about a different Jane Doe, is a false assertion — an ethical-line violation, not just a technical error.
- ⚠️ **The Wikipedia line in the example above is there to show the shape of a `sameAs` array, not to suggest a candidate should have one.** Most first-time down-ballot candidates do not meet Wikipedia's notability threshold, and creating or editing an article about yourself violates its conflict-of-interest policy (§3.4). If an article legitimately exists and is about this person, link it. Otherwise delete the line. Do not go make one.
- Use `jobTitle: "Candidate for..."` — do **not** use `Person.hasOccupation` with a held-office title unless the office is actually held. Claiming incumbency in markup is a factual misrepresentation.
- `legalName` should match the filed committee name exactly; most jurisdictions require the committee ID in disclaimers anyway.
- Timezone offsets included per Google's `dateModified`/`datePublished` guidance.
- **UNVERIFIED:** whether `ProfilePage` requires `dateCreated`/`dateModified` for eligibility. I did not fetch the ProfilePage doc this session — check https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page before shipping. Including them is harmless either way.

## 3. What actually correlates with AI citation (2025–2026 evidence)

### 3.1 Off-site brand signals dominate — (b) STUDY

**Ahrefs, 75,000 brands, Spearman correlations with AI Overview brand visibility**
https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/

| Factor | Correlation |
| --- | --- |
| Branded web mentions | **0.664** |
| Branded anchors | 0.527 |
| Branded search volume | 0.392 |
| Domain Rating | 0.326 |
| Number of backlinks | **0.218** |
| Branded ad traffic | 0.216 |

- Web mentions correlate **~3× more strongly than backlinks**.
- Top-quartile brands by web mentions average **169** AI Overview mentions vs **14** for the next quartile down — "up to 10X more."
- **26% of brands studied had zero AI Overview mentions.**

**Ahrefs follow-up across ChatGPT / AI Mode / AI Overviews, same 75k brands**
https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations/

| Factor | Correlation |
| --- | --- |
| YouTube mentions | **~0.737** (highest of any factor tested) |
| Branded web mentions | 0.66–0.71 |
| Branded anchors (AI Mode) | 0.628 |
| Branded search volume (AI Mode) | 0.466 |
| Branded search volume (ChatGPT) | 0.352 |
| Domain Rating (ChatGPT) | 0.266 |
| **Number of site pages** | **~0.194** |

Two findings matter enormously for a down-ballot campaign:

1. **Publishing more pages is nearly uncorrelated with AI visibility (~0.194).** This kills the "build a 200-page issue site" strategy — and conveniently, that strategy is also the one that risks Google's scaled content abuse policy.
2. **YouTube is the single strongest correlate.** For a candidate, that translates to: post the candidate forum footage, the stump speech, the "why I'm running" video. Caveat: correlation, and YouTube is Google-owned, so self-preference is a plausible confound (**(c)** on the mechanism).

**Cross-platform agreement:** AI Overviews & AI Mode 0.821, AI Overviews & ChatGPT 0.749, AI Mode & ChatGPT 0.769. The platforms largely surface the *same* entities — so there is no need for per-platform strategies.

⚠️ **Correlational, vendor-run, on commercial brands** — not candidates, not causal. Treat as directional.

### 3.2 Content structure and chunk-level absorption — (b) STUDY, and the best one available

**"From Citation Selection to Citation Absorption" (arXiv, April 26, 2026)**
https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25707v1 — 602 controlled prompts, 21,143 valid citations, 18,151 fetched pages, 72 features, across ChatGPT / Google AI Overview+Gemini / Perplexity.

This paper is unusually rigorous for the GEO space — it separates *citation selection* (were you cited) from *citation absorption* (did your text shape the answer), and it publishes a claim-level self-audit explicitly refusing causal claims.

**Citation breadth vs. depth:**

| Platform | Mean citations/prompt | Mean page influence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ChatGPT | 6.88 | **0.2713** |
| Google | 12.06 | 0.0584 |
| Perplexity | **16.35** | 0.0646 |

Perplexity cites the most sources; ChatGPT cites fewest but leans on each far more heavily.

**Evidence genre → mean influence uplift (the actionable table):**

| Feature present | True mean | False mean | Relative difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contains comparison content | 0.1389 | 0.0894 | **+55.28%** |
| Contains how-to content | 0.1296 | 0.0918 | **+41.20%** |
| **Q&A format** | 0.0947 | 0.1005 | **−5.74%** |

> "Pages containing code, numbers, definitions, comparisons, or how-to content show higher mean influence. **Q&A format shows the opposite direction.** The likely mechanism is that evidence genres create reusable support units, while Q&A formatting is only a surface wrapper."

**This directly contradicts the most common piece of GEO advice in circulation.** "Reformat everything as Q&A" is not supported.

**Strongest independent correlations with influence:**
- LLM relevance score: **r = 0.4322**
- Answer-citation embedding similarity: r = 0.3561
- LLM content quality: r = 0.2917
- Question-citation embedding similarity: r = 0.2548

> "Page word count and structural markers matter, but **semantic fit is stronger than a simple length signal.**"

**On structure specifically:**
> "High-influence pages are not merely longer. They contain more headings, more paragraphs, denser list structures, higher semantic similarity, and higher LLM-rated relevance and quality."

**Domain type → absorption:** encyclopedia pages **0.2144** mean influence vs news_media **0.0726**. News gets *selected* often but *absorbed* shallowly.

**Platform-specific profiles:** ChatGPT weights LLM relevance most; Google weights embedding similarity and definition markers; Perplexity combines relevance with **heading count and length**.

**The authors' own caveat, which the skill should reproduce honestly:**
> "It cannot isolate whether headings cause absorption, whether high-quality pages simply tend to have more headings, or whether both are downstream of a latent editorial-quality variable."

Authority still gates entry: median Domain Rating of cited sources was 526–592.

### 3.3 Freshness — (b) STUDY

**Ahrefs, 16.975 million cited URLs across 7 platforms**
https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/

- AI-cited URLs average **1,064 days** old vs **1,432 days** for organic top-10 → **25.7% fresher**
- **AI Overviews cite the oldest** content (avg >1,432 days, last updated 1,047 days ago)
- **ChatGPT cites the newest** (in-text refs 1,023 days; citations 958 days)
- Perplexity 1,166 → Gemini 1,118 → Copilot 1,056 → ChatGPT
- Perplexity and ChatGPT order in-text references newest→oldest

⚠️ **Debunking note (b):** a widely circulated "4.3× freshness preference" figure is not supported. The verified figure is 25.7%. https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-search-citation-ranking-factors-2026-data-study

**Campaign implication:** freshness is real but modest, and *the direction is right* for a campaign — an active campaign naturally produces fresh content. It does not justify fake `dateModified` bumps, which would be both a lie and a spam-policy risk.

### 3.4 Wikipedia, Reddit, and platform source mixes — (b) STUDY

**5W synthesis of 680M+ tracked citations** https://www.5wpr.com/research/state-of-ai-citations-2026/
- **Perplexity: Reddit ≈ 46.7% of top-10 source share.** Most community-skewed platform by a wide margin. Most-cited domains skew to review aggregators (G2, Gartner, NerdWallet, PCMag, TripAdvisor, Yelp).
- **Google AI Overviews: ~43% of citations link to Google-owned properties**, including YouTube.
- **AI Overviews have the strongest tie to classic ranking: 93.67% of citations link to at least one top-10 organic result** — but only 4.5% of AI Overview URLs directly match a Page-1 organic URL, implying Google pulls deeper pages from authoritative domains.
- Google AI Mode cites LinkedIn in ~15% of responses (per Semrush).

**Profound, 11.84 billion citations, 8,061 categories, Apr 16 – Jul 16, 2026** https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/where-do-ai-citations-come-from
- ChatGPT is the most earned-media-heavy: **30%** of citations are third-party editorial/institutional
- AI Overviews the least: **17.0%**
- AI Overviews 15.3% and AI Mode 14.4% social citations — ~1.3× ChatGPT's rate, ~4× Copilot's

⚠️ **Conflicting numbers, flagged:** the "% of AI Overview citations from top-10 pages" figure appears as **76%**, **93.67%**, and **38%** across sources. These likely measure different things (any-citation vs. all-citations vs. exact-URL-match). **Do not cite a single number for this.** Mark as UNVERIFIED at the specific-figure level; the *direction* — classic ranking strongly gates AI Overview citation — replicates across all three.

**On Wikipedia specifically:** ChatGPT favors encyclopedic sources, and the arXiv paper measured encyclopedia pages at the highest absorption (0.2144). **But this is not actionable and the skill must say so.** Wikipedia notability standards exclude most first-time down-ballot candidates, and attempting to create or edit one's own Wikipedia article violates Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest policy. **This is a hard "do not" for the skill.** The legitimate analogues are Ballotpedia (which *does* cover down-ballot races and accepts candidate-submitted survey responses through an official process), Vote Smart, and League of Women Voters / VOTE411 guides.

### 3.5 Meta-analysis — (c) SPECULATION, but well-constructed

Cyrus Shepard / Zyppy, May 7, 2026: synthesized 54 experiments, patents, and case studies into 23 scored factors, weighted by repeatability, evidence strength, and official platform support. Top-scoring factors reported as: crawlability (9.5/10), search rank (9.4), fan-out rank (9.3), preview control (9.2), query-answer match (9.2). Via https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-search-citation-ranking-factors-2026-data-study

Labeled **(c)** because it is a weighted synthesis of heterogeneous sources, not primary data. I did not access the Zyppy original — **UNVERIFIED at the primary-source level.** The ordering is notable mainly because it agrees with the vendor documentation: reachable, already-ranking, query-matched, previewable.

### 3.6 Primary-source citation

**UNVERIFIED.** I found no study isolating "pages that cite primary sources get cited more." The nearest evidence is the arXiv finding that pages containing numbers, definitions, and comparisons show higher absorption — which is adjacent but not the same claim. Any assertion that linking to primary sources improves AI citation should be labeled **(c) SPECULATION**. It remains good practice on the merits (it is honest and verifiable), just not on demonstrated citation grounds.

## 4. llms.txt — the straight answer

**Status: not consumed by any major answer engine. Do not sell it as a visibility tactic.**

### Vendor positions — (a) VENDOR

| Company | Position |
| --- | --- |
| **Google** | Explicit: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown to appear in Google Search... as **Google Search itself doesn't use them.**" A June 15, 2026 changelog entry added the clarification that these files "aren't needed for Google Search (and **won't negatively or positively impact your visibility or rankings**), it's fine if you want to maintain these files for other services or systems that use them." |
| **OpenAI** | Crawler documentation never mentions llms.txt; directs site owners to robots.txt (OAI-SearchBot for search, GPTBot for training). Publishes one for its *own* API docs. |
| **Anthropic** | Same pattern: crawler docs cover robots.txt only. Publishes one for its own developer docs. |
| **Perplexity** | No support announced; bot documentation silent on the file. (It does publish one for its own docs.) |
| **Microsoft** | No support announced. |

Google's John Mueller, June 2025: "**FWIW no AI system currently uses llms.txt**" — and separately characterized it as "a temporary crutch." Via https://originality.ai/blog/llms-txt-tracking-study

### Server-log evidence — (b) STUDY

**Ahrefs, 137,000 domains** https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/
- 28% of domains publish an llms.txt
- **97% of those files received zero traffic in May 2026** — nothing fetched them at all
- Anthropic's *Claude-Code* (a coding agent) out-fetched every AI retrieval bot, assistant, and training crawler

**EZY Research, 83 sites with server logs, 12 weeks** https://www.ezy.ai/research/do-ai-bots-read-llms-txt

| Crawler | robots.txt fetches | llms.txt fetches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OpenAI (GPTBot family) | 3,990 | **7** |
| Anthropic (ClaudeBot) | 3,120 | **9** |
| PerplexityBot | 775 | **0** |
| Googlebot | 5,125 | 67 |
| **Meta-ExternalAgent** | 172 | **193** |

The one genuine exception: **Meta's crawler fetches llms.txt more often than robots.txt.** Everyone else is in the single digits against thousands.

**OtterlyAI, 90 days:** of 62,100+ AI bot visits to a site with llms.txt at root, **84 requests (~0.1%)** went to the file.

**MaxAEO, 19 customer log sets, Feb–Apr 2026:** major AI crawlers requested `/llms.txt` on only 3 of 19 sites — **41 requests against ~1.1 million AI-crawler page fetches.** Most hits came from SEO tools and unidentified scrapers.

⚠️ Adoption *is* growing — Originality.AI counted 36,120 files across 3M scanned sites, 8.8× year over year — but adoption by publishers is not consumption by engines.

### Recommendation for the skill

**Do not include llms.txt in the core path.** Rationale: it costs a first-time candidate's limited attention, no answer engine consumes it, and Google states it has literally zero effect either way. If included at all, make it an explicitly-labeled optional extra with the honest framing: *"Meta's crawler reads this; no one else meaningfully does. ~15 minutes, no measurable benefit today."*

The one contrary data point (**(c)**, single-operator, n=3 sites, self-reported): one practitioner logged PerplexityBot fetching it 47 times over 60 days. This conflicts with EZY's 0 and MaxAEO's near-zero at larger n. https://geotrackerai.com/community/watched-our-server-logs-for-60-days-to-see-who-actually — **treat as noise, not signal.**

## 5. AI crawlers and robots.txt — the catastrophe-prevention section

**This is the highest-stakes section.** Blocking a retrieval crawler silently removes a candidate from that engine's answers with no error message and no notification.

### 5.1 The critical distinction

Every major AI company now splits its crawlers into **three** functional roles:

1. **Training crawlers** — collect content to train foundation models. Blocking has **no effect on visibility**. This is the one a privacy- or copyright-conscious campaign may legitimately choose to block.
2. **Search/retrieval crawlers** — build the index the assistant searches at answer time. **Blocking these makes the candidate invisible in that assistant's answers.** ⚠️ Never block these on a campaign site.
3. **User-triggered fetchers** — fetch a page in real time because a specific user asked. Blocking breaks a request a real person made. Several vendors state robots.txt may not apply to these.

### 5.2 The complete table — all (a) VENDOR

| robots.txt token | Company | Role | Blocking effect | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 5.3 ⚠️ The Google trap that will bite campaigns

**There is no `Google-AIOverviews` token, and `Google-Extended` does not control AI Overviews.**

`Google-Extended` controls **Gemini training and Gemini grounding**. AI Overviews and AI Mode are Google Search features, gated by `Googlebot` plus the snippet-eligibility rule from §1.2. A campaign that blocks `Google-Extended` believing it opts out of AI Overviews has done nothing to AI Overviews — and a campaign that blocks `Googlebot` to "keep AI out" has removed itself from Google Search entirely.

### 5.4 ⚠️ Microsoft is the exception: robots.txt is not the control surface

Microsoft governs AI usage through **page-level meta directives**, not robots.txt:

| Directive | Effect (Microsoft's wording) |
| --- | --- |
| *(neither tag)* | Content "may be included in Bing Chat answers... site content may be used in training our generative AI foundation models" |
| `nocache` | "Display only URL/Snippet/Title in Chat or Copilot. Use only URLs, Titles and Snippets for training Microsoft's generative AI foundation models." |
| `noarchive` | "Do not link in Chat and Copilot. Do not use the content for training Microsoft's generative AI foundation models." |
| both | "we will treat it as NOCACHE" |

Source: https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/september-2023/Announcing-new-options-for-webmasters-to-control-usage-of-their-content-in-Bing-Chat

⚠️ **`noarchive` is a silent Copilot killer.** Many CMS "privacy" and "security hardening" plugins set `<meta name="robots" content="noarchive">` by default. On a campaign site this removes the candidate from Copilot answers entirely, with no warning. **The skill should audit for this explicitly.**

Bing also supports `data-nosnippet` at the element level as of October 2025 — content stays indexed and ranks, but is excluded from snippets and AI summaries. https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/October-2025/Bing-Introduces-Support-for-the-data-nosnippet-HTML-Attribute

Maximum visibility across every answer engine. A campaign is publishing *in order to be found* — there is rarely a reason to block anything.

**Audit checklist the skill should run:**
1. `robots.txt` returns HTTP 200 (not 404, not 403, not a soft-404 HTML page)
2. No `Disallow: /` under `User-agent: *` — the classic staging-site leak
3. No `noindex` on the homepage, bio, issues, or events pages
4. **No `noarchive` or `nocache` meta anywhere** (Copilot killer, §5.4)
5. No `nosnippet` / `max-snippet:0` — breaks Google AI feature eligibility (§1.2)
6. No `data-nosnippet` wrapping main content
7. Cloudflare/WAF "Block AI Scrapers" or "AI Labyrinth" toggle **off** — ⚠️ this is one-click and increasingly on by default at hosts; it silently overrides a permissive robots.txt
8. Site renders core content without JavaScript execution
9. Verified in Search Console, opted into generative AI features

**Verification note (a) VENDOR:** user-agent strings are trivially spoofable. All four vendors publish IP JSON files for verification: `openai.com/searchbot.json`, `openai.com/gptbot.json`, `openai.com/chatgpt-user.json`, `perplexity.com/perplexitybot.json`, `perplexity.com/perplexity-user.json`; Anthropic publishes an IP list; Google supports reverse-DNS to `crawl-*.googlebot.com`. Match on the **token substring** (`GPTBot`, `OAI-SearchBot`), never a pinned version — versions increment (OAI-SearchBot has gone 1.0 → 1.4).

**Propagation:** OpenAI documents "~24 hours from a site's robots.txt update for our systems to adjust." Perplexity: "up to 24 hours." A mistake takes a day to fix.

## 6. Chunk-level retrievability — synthesis

⚠️ **Read this caveat first.** Google explicitly says chunking is unnecessary and that there is "no ideal page length" **(a) VENDOR**. The arXiv study found structural markers correlate with absorption but explicitly declined to claim causation **(b) STUDY**. Most published "chunking" advice is **(c) SPECULATION.** Below, each item is labeled with its actual support.

### One question per page — **(c) SPECULATION, and Google pushes back**

Google directly warns that creating pages per query variation can violate scaled content abuse (§1.3), and Ahrefs found page count nearly uncorrelated with AI visibility (~0.194) **(b)**.

**Recommendation:** one page per *substantive position* (housing, water, schools), not per *phrasing*. A dozen genuine issue pages, not eighty query-variant pages. This is also the honest structure: it reflects what the candidate actually believes rather than what a keyword tool emitted.

### Answer-first paragraphs — **(c) SPECULATION**

No vendor documents this and no study I found isolates it. The nearest support is the arXiv finding that answer-citation embedding similarity correlates at r=0.3561 **(b)** — front-loading the direct answer plausibly raises passage-level similarity, but that is inference.

**Recommendation:** worth doing because it is good writing for voters. Do not present it to users as evidence-backed.

### Heading structure — **(b) STUDY + (a) VENDOR, weakly**

Google: "headings that provide a clear structure to navigate content" **(a)**. arXiv: high-influence pages "contain more headings, more paragraphs, denser list structures," and heading count is one of Perplexity's stronger signals specifically **(b)**. But the authors explicitly refuse to claim headings *cause* absorption.

**Recommendation:** one `<h1>`, descriptive `<h2>`s phrased as the question a voter would actually ask, self-contained sections. Low cost, plausible benefit, real accessibility payoff.

### Evidence density — **(b) STUDY, the strongest structural finding available**

Comparisons **+55.28%**, how-to **+41.20%**, and definitions/numbers all positive; Q&A wrapper **−5.74%**.

**Recommendation — this is the highest-value item in this section.** Instead of "Jane supports better schools," write the specific number, the specific vote, the specific date, the specific dollar figure, with a link to the primary record. This is simultaneously what the data supports *and* what accountable political communication looks like. There is no tension between the tactic and the ethics.

### Date stamping — **(b) STUDY, modest effect**

25.7% freshness advantage (§3.3). Use visible human-readable dates plus ISO 8601 `datePublished`/`dateModified` with timezone offsets **(a)**.

⚠️ **Hard ethical line:** update `dateModified` only on genuine substantive edits. Automated date-bumping is both deceptive and a documented Google spam concern.

### Author attribution — **(a) VENDOR for the mechanics**

Google's author markup rules are documented and specific (§2.5). Whether authorship affects AI citation is **UNVERIFIED** — no study I found isolates it.

**Recommendation:** attribute position papers to the candidate by name with a `url` to the bio page. For a candidate this is not an SEO tactic, it is basic accountability — the voter should know who is speaking.

### Internal linking — **UNVERIFIED**

I found no vendor guidance or study specifically on internal linking and AI citation. Standard crawl-discovery reasoning applies (pages must be reachable), which is **(a)** by implication from Google's crawlability requirement, but any stronger claim is **(c)**.

### What the evidence says actually matters most, in order

1. **Be crawlable and snippet-eligible** — (a) VENDOR, hard gate, §5
2. **Be mentioned elsewhere by real sources** — (b) STUDY, strongest correlate at 0.664, §3.1
3. **Rank in classic search** — (a) VENDOR + (b) STUDY, §1.1 / §3.4
4. **Pack pages with specific, verifiable evidence** — (b) STUDY, +55% / +41%, §3.2
5. **Keep content current and honestly dated** — (b) STUDY, 25.7%, §3.3
6. **Clear headings and structure** — (b) weak + (a) weak, §6
7. **Structured data** — (a) VENDOR says optional; real value is identity disambiguation, §2.4
8. **llms.txt** — no evidence of consumption; skip, §4

## 7. What this means for the skill's ethical posture

Worth stating explicitly in the skill's documentation, because the evidence is unusually favorable here: **the manipulative tactics are also the ineffective ones.**

- Google's own docs say inauthentic mentions "isn't as helpful as it might seem" and that spam systems target them **(a)**
- Scaled query-variant page generation is a named spam policy violation **(a)**
- Ahrefs measured page volume at ~0.194 correlation — near zero **(b)**
- The arXiv paper found the most-recommended formatting hack (Q&A wrapping) slightly *negative* **(b)**
- Meanwhile the top correlate is genuine third-party mentions **(b)**, and the top structural factor is specific verifiable evidence **(b)**

For a first-time down-ballot candidate the entire honest playbook is: **be findable** (robots.txt correct, indexable, snippet-eligible), **be specific** (real numbers, real dates, real records, primary-source links), **be identifiable** (consistent name/office/district, `sameAs` to Ballotpedia and official filings), and **be genuinely covered** (local press, candidate forums, LWV/VOTE411, Ballotpedia's official candidate survey).
