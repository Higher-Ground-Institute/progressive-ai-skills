# State candidate statement programs

**All facts verified 2026-08-04**, for the November 3, 2026 general election. Labels:
**(VENDOR)** for official or statutory sources, **(SECONDARY)** for third party, **UNVERIFIED**
for anything not confirmed.

**Used by:** `placement-writer`, `canonical-presence`
**Everything else — Ballotpedia, Vote411, questionnaires, county guides:**
[`venues.md`](venues.md)

## The three-way branch

A state candidate statement is one of three things, and which one determines whether it is
worth any of a campaign's time:

1. **Free and open** — do it immediately. Arizona is the only verified example, and its
   deadline has effectively passed for 2026.
2. **Paid** — a real budget decision. Oregon at $750 for a legislative seat, Alaska, California
   pro rata.
3. **Unavailable** — most states. Their voter pamphlets carry ballot measures only, or there is
   no pamphlet at all.

**Do not assume a state has a program because a neighboring state does.** Of the eighteen
states checked, five have one, four verifiably do not, and nine could not be confirmed.

## Oregon — paid, and the only one still comfortably open

The best-documented program of the eighteen.

**State Voters' Pamphlet, state legislative:** **$750** fee, **325 words**, or file by petition
with **200 valid signatures** instead of the fee.
([manual](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/documents/vpmanual.pdf), VENDOR)

Full fee schedule, all at 325 words: President/VP $3,500 (500 sigs) · US Senator or statewide
$3,000 (500) · US Representative $2,500 (300) · **State Senator or Representative $750 (200)**
· Circuit Court Judge $600 (200).

**2026 general deadlines**
([quick guide](https://sos.oregon.gov/elections/Documents/Voters-Pamphlet-Filing-Quick-Guide.pdf),
VENDOR):

- First day to file by fee or completed petition: 2026-07-06
- **Last day to file: 2026-08-25, 5:00:00 pm**
- Last day to submit signatures for guaranteed verification: 2026-08-11, 5:00:00 pm

**An amendment rule worth knowing before choosing fee versus petition:** if you pay the fee,
*"Statements and Arguments may still be amended up until the deadline."* If you file by
petition, *"The statement or argument cannot be amended after this point."* (VENDOR)

Filing is electronic, through ORESTAR.

**County candidates use a separate county pamphlet** with its own filing. The state guide warns:
*"These deadlines do not apply to county voters' pamphlets. Contact your county elections
official."* Per the state elections calendar the 2026 county window is 2026-07-06 to
2026-08-25 (ORS 251.335 / OAR 165-022-0010), but confirm with the county.

## Washington — free, state deadline gone, some counties still open

**Word limits are statutory** ([RCW 29A.32.121](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=29A.32.121),
VENDOR): state representative **100 words**; state senator, superior court judge, court of
appeals judge, supreme court justice, and statewide offices other than governor **200 words**;
president, vice president, US senator, US representative, and governor **300 words**.

**Cost: free.** There is a candidacy filing fee — one percent of the office's annual salary,
with a signature-petition alternative for candidates *"without sufficient assets or income"* —
but no separate charge for the pamphlet statement.

**⚠️ The state deadline has passed and there is no second chance**
([2026 State Candidates Guide](https://www.sos.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2026-02/StateCandidatesGuide2026.pdf),
VENDOR):

> "The deadline to submit Voters' Pamphlet information is 5 p.m. on Tuesday, May 19, 2026. No
> submissions or corrections after the deadline."

> "Whether you appear in the August Primary or go directly to the General Election, you will
> not receive another opportunity to submit or edit a profile."

**County and local candidates file with the county, separately, and some windows are still
open.** Limits come from RCW 29A.32.230 and deadlines from RCW 29A.32.125, but both are
implemented per-county. King County's general-election deadline is **2026-08-07, 5:00 pm**.
See [`venues.md`](venues.md) for verified county examples.

## Arizona — free, unusually generous, and the deadline is now

Run by the **Citizens Clean Elections Commission**, not the Secretary of State. That is why
campaigns miss it.

**Every candidate on the ballot is eligible, not only publicly financed ones**
([candidate guide](https://storageccec.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/public/docs/1414-Candidate-Guide-2026-fullguide-1-3-18-26.pdf),
VENDOR):

> "Participating and traditional candidates may supply the Commission with a picture,
> statement, and bio for inclusion in the pamphlets. All candidates who will appear on either
> the primary or general election ballot may submit."

Non-submission is printed: the pamphlet lists every candidate, and *"For any candidate who does
not submit a message... the document shall include with the candidate's listing the words 'no
statement submitted'."*

**New for 2026, countywide offices are included** — *"for the first time ever"*
([Clean Elections](https://www.azcleanelections.gov/en/arizona-elections/voter-education-guide),
VENDOR). School board and municipal candidates are still excluded.

**Free**, paid from the clean elections fund. **200-word statement, 100-word bio**, both
hard-truncated: *"Statements will be cutoff at the 200-word mark... The statement and bio word
count will be verified by Microsoft Word."* No changes after submission. A primary statement
carries forward to the general automatically if no new one is submitted.

**⚠️ Official sources give two different deadlines.** The same Clean Elections candidate guide
says **August 5, 2026** in its summary table and **midnight August 3, 2026** in its body text.
The 2026 workshop deck says August 5 with *"dates subject to change."* Two circulating versions
of the guide also give different **primary** dates (July 21 vs. August 4, 2026).

**Contact the Commission directly rather than trusting any of these.** As of 2026-08-04 one
date has passed and the other is tomorrow.

## Alaska — paid, deadline passed

The Official Election Pamphlet is published only for the general and mailed to every registered
household. *"Candidates and recognized political parties must pay to appear in the OEP."*
([Division of Elections](https://www.elections.alaska.gov/publications/official-election-pamphlet/),
VENDOR)

**Limits, per AS 15.58.030:** a position statement of **250 words or less** and a biographical
statement of **150 words or less**, on one page with a photograph, *"clearly identified with the
words 'provided and paid for by the candidate.'"*

**Eligible offices:** US Senator, US Representative, Governor, Lieutenant Governor, **state
senator, state representative**. Borough, county, and school board candidates are not covered.

**Deadline was 2026-07-22, 5:00 pm** (2026-06-22 for candidates wanting Alaska Native language
translation in specified districts).

The fee amount is set by 6 AAC 25.690 and is **UNVERIFIED**.

## California — paid, and two separate programs people confuse

### County and local nonpartisan offices

Governed by [Elections Code §13307](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=ELEC&sectionNum=13307.)
(VENDOR). **200 words**, which the local agency may raise to **400**. No party affiliation in
the statement.

**Paid, pro rata**, in advance, as a condition of inclusion. The estimate is not binding — the
agency may bill a shortfall or refund an excess afterward.

**An indigency waiver exists and is genuinely useful.**
[§13309](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=ELEC&sectionNum=13309)
lets a candidate who *"alleges to be indigent and unable to pay in advance"* submit a statement
of financial worth; if found indigent, *"the local agency shall print and mail the statement."*
The agency may still bill the actual pro rata share after the election.

**Deadline is tied to nomination filing, not the election date:** the statement is filed *"when
the candidate's nomination papers are returned for filing"* and *"may be withdrawn, but not
changed"* until 5 pm the next working day after the nomination period closes. For the Nov 3
2026 general this has almost certainly passed. Exact dates are per-jurisdiction, UNVERIFIED.

### State Senate and Assembly — conditional on accepting spending limits

**250 words**, purchasable only by a candidate who accepts the voluntary expenditure limits.
[Gov. Code §85601(c)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=85601)
(VENDOR): *"The statement may not make any reference to any opponent of the candidate."*

You must file **Form 501**, the Candidate Intention Statement, accepting the limits before
submitting a statement
([SoS](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/candidate-statements), VENDOR).

Prop 34 ceilings, effective 2025-01-01 through 2026-12-31
([FPPC](https://www.fppc.ca.gov/learn/campaign-rules/state-contribution-limits-and-voluntary-expenditure-ceilings/),
VENDOR):

| Office | Primary | General |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly | $784,000 | $1,373,000 |
| Senate | $1,177,000 | $1,765,000 |

**Multi-county districts:** file with each county separately. The versions do not have to
match, you are not obligated to file in every county, and *"Procedures, requirements, fees, and
formats for candidates' statements may vary between counties."* (SECONDARY, restating
EC §13307.5)

## States with no program

**Montana.** [MCA 13-27-401](https://archive.legmt.gov/bills/mca/title_0130/chapter_0270/part_0040/section_0010/0130-0270-0040-0010.html)
sits in the chapter on statewide ballot issues and enumerates pamphlet contents as ballot
title, fiscal statement, full text, ballot form, arguments for and against, and rebuttals. **No
candidate content is authorized.** (VENDOR)

**Colorado.** The Blue Book is measures-only by statute — text, title, and impartial analysis of
each measure, plus arguments and a fiscal assessment. The only candidate-adjacent content is
judicial performance evaluations, written by the Office of Judicial Performance Evaluation
rather than by candidates.
([General Assembly](https://leg.colorado.gov/initiatives/ballot-information-booklet-blue-book),
VENDOR)

**Nevada.** No program for legislative or local candidates. One judicial exception: under SB418
(2023), non-incumbent judicial candidates must file a questionnaire including *"a candidate
statement not to exceed 200 words,"* posted verbatim by the election official. (VENDOR) You
will encounter the claim that "Nevada has a candidate questionnaire" — it is true only for
judges.

**Massachusetts.** The *Information for Voters* booklet is ballot-questions-only: text, effect
of a yes or no vote, a summary, and arguments. No candidate section.
([SoC](https://www.sec.state.ma.us/divisions/elections/download/research-and-statistics/IFV_2024_Large.pdf),
VENDOR — verified against the 2024 edition; the 2026 edition following the same statutory
structure is a reasonable inference but formally UNVERIFIED.)

## Not verified — nine states

**Ohio, Texas, Michigan, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, Georgia, Virginia, Minnesota,
Wisconsin.**

The working expectation is that none runs a candidate statement program for legislative or
local candidates. **That expectation is not verified and must not be published as fact.**

Two secondary sources were found and both are unusable. A 2001 Connecticut legislative research
report lists Michigan and Montana as states whose guides include candidate information — it is
25 years old and demonstrably wrong about Montana, whose current statute contradicts it
outright. Ohio's Secretary of State offers a sample-ballot lookup with no candidate-authored
content, which is weak negative evidence at best.

**To resolve one:** open the Secretary of State or State Board of Elections **candidate manual**
for the 2026 cycle — not the voter-facing pages — and search it for "pamphlet," "statement," and
"voter guide." Then add the finding to the table above with a source URL and a date.

Absence of evidence in a web search is not evidence of absence, and a reference document should
not launder one into the other.

## How to check a state not listed here

1. Find the Secretary of State or State Board of Elections **candidate manual** for 2026. Search
   for "pamphlet," "candidate statement," and "voter guide." These programs are named
   inconsistently across states — "voters' pamphlet," "candidate statement," "voter education
   guide," "official election pamphlet."
2. If the state has one, find the statute. The word limit and the deadline are usually set in
   law, which makes them reliable and quotable.
3. **Check whether it is administered by a body other than the Secretary of State.** Arizona's
   runs through the Clean Elections Commission, which is exactly why candidates miss it.
4. Check whether county and local candidates are covered or excluded. Being covered for the
   legislature says nothing about school board.
5. Determine the cost, and whether an indigency or petition alternative exists.
6. If the deadline has passed, record it as a dated next-cycle calendar entry rather than a
   dead end. Most of these are annual or biennial and predictable.
7. **Add what you find here**, with a source URL and the date you checked.
