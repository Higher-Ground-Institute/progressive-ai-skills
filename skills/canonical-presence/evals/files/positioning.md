# Positioning — Maya Ellison for Ashfield County Commission, District 3

> Fixture for skill evaluation. Ashfield County, Millbrook, Maya Ellison, and every URL and
> ordinance number below are invented. Do not treat anything here as a real record.

## Message

### One sentence

Ashfield County has approved 3,100 new housing units and built 210 of them, and Maya Ellison
is a building inspector who knows exactly where the other 2,890 are stuck.

### The paragraph

Ashfield County has a permitting backlog, not a zoning problem. The Board approved 3,100
units of housing between 2021 and 2025 and only 210 have been built, because a permit that is
supposed to take 45 days is taking 287. Maya Ellison spent eleven years as a county building
inspector and has read the file on most of those projects. She is running to fix the part of
the housing shortage the county actually controls.

### Why not the other one

The incumbent has voted for every housing approval and against every staffing increase in the
permitting office. Those two votes cancel out, and the record shows it.

## Topics to own

### 1. Housing permit backlog — slug: `housing-permits`

- **Salience evidence:** Median residential permit turnaround reached 287 days in Q1 2026, up
  from 45 days in 2019 ([Ashfield County Development Services Quarterly Report
  Q1-2026](https://devservices.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/reports/2026-q1), retrieved
  2026-06-10). 41 residents signed up for public comment at the March 12 Board meeting on the
  Millbrook Commons delay ([minutes](https://ashfieldcounty.example.gov/minutes/2026-03-12),
  retrieved 2026-06-10).
- **Earned standing:** Eleven years as an Ashfield County building inspector, 2013–2024.
  Ellison personally inspected 1,400+ residential projects and can describe the bottleneck
  step by step.
- **Contested space:** No one has published an explanation of why the backlog exists. The
  Millbrook Ledger has covered individual stalled projects four times since January but has
  never reported the turnaround figure. The county's own quarterly report publishes the
  number in a PDF appendix with no commentary.
- **The case against including this topic:** Permitting is procedural and unglamorous, and
  "hire three plan reviewers" is a hard thing to put on a yard sign.

### 2. Millbrook school bond — slug: `school-bond`

- **Salience evidence:** Measure B, a $94 million school facilities bond, is on the same
  November 3 ballot ([Ashfield County Elections, measures
  filed](https://elections.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/2026/measures), retrieved 2026-06-10).
- **Earned standing:** Ellison's two children attend Millbrook schools. She served on the
  2023 facilities advisory committee that produced the underlying assessment.
- **Contested space:** The bond campaign publishes advocacy material and the opposition
  committee publishes a one-page flyer. Nobody has published the actual condition assessment
  in readable form.
- **The case against including this topic:** A commissioner does not control the school
  district budget, and taking a position on a bond invites a fight that is not this race.

### 3. Data center water usage — slug: `data-center-water`

- **Salience evidence:** Northgate Partners has applied to build a 340,000 sq ft data center
  on the former Ashfield Fairgrounds site; the application projects 1.1 million gallons per
  day of cooling water ([application AC-2026-0331, Planning
  Commission](https://planning.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/applications/AC-2026-0331),
  retrieved 2026-06-14). Public comment period closes 2026-09-15.
- **Earned standing:** Ellison inspected the fairgrounds site in 2019 and 2022.
- **Contested space:** Wide open. No official, outlet, or organization in the county has
  published anything about the water figure.
- **The case against including this topic:** The campaign does not have a position yet — see
  `## No position yet`.

## Positions of record

### Housing permit backlog — slug: `housing-permits`

- **Position, one sentence:** Fund three additional plan reviewers and a permit tracking
  system in the FY2027 budget, and publish turnaround times monthly.
- **What they would actually do:** Move an amendment at the Board's FY2027 budget markup in
  April 2027 adding $412,000 to Development Services — three reviewer positions at $118,000
  fully loaded plus a $58,000 tracking system — offset from the $2.1 million unallocated
  general fund balance.
- **Evidence:**
  - Median residential permit turnaround was 287 days in Q1 2026, against a 45-day statutory
    target ([Development Services Q1-2026
    report](https://devservices.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/reports/2026-q1), retrieved
    2026-06-10).
  - Development Services has 4 plan reviewer positions budgeted, the same number as in 2014,
    against a permit volume that has risen 190% ([FY2026 adopted
    budget](https://ashfieldcounty.example.gov/budget/fy2026), p. 143, retrieved 2026-06-10).
  - Neighboring Calder County, with a comparable population, runs 61-day median turnaround
    with 9 reviewers ([Calder County Development Services 2025 annual
    report](https://calder.example.gov/ds/2025-annual), retrieved 2026-06-11).
  - 3,100 units approved 2021–2025; 210 completed
    ([minutes](https://ashfieldcounty.example.gov/minutes/2026-03-12), retrieved 2026-06-10).
- **Uncertainty:** Whether three reviewers is the right number. The 2023 workload study
  recommended between two and five and the county has never run the analysis again. Ellison
  would commission it in the first quarter.
- **The strongest objection, and the answer:** *Adding staff to a slow office often just adds
  slow staff.* Fair — which is why the monthly published turnaround number matters more than
  the headcount. If turnaround does not move within two budget cycles, the problem is process,
  not staffing, and the county should say so publicly.
- **Conviction:** settled
- **Approved:** yes — 2026-07-28

### Millbrook school bond — slug: `school-bond`

- **Position, one sentence:** Support Measure B, because the 2023 facilities assessment found
  four Millbrook schools with roofs past their service life and the district has no capital
  reserve.
- **What they would actually do:** A county commissioner does not vote on the bond. Ellison
  would endorse it publicly and would support the county's share of the joint-use field
  agreement that depends on it.
- **Evidence:**
  - The 2023 facilities condition assessment rated 4 of 11 Millbrook schools' roofs "past
    service life," with a combined $31 million replacement estimate
    ([assessment](https://millbrookschools.example.org/facilities/2023-assessment), p. 12,
    retrieved 2026-06-13).
  - The district's capital reserve balance was $1.4 million as of the FY2026 audit
    ([audit](https://millbrookschools.example.org/finance/fy2026-audit), retrieved
    2026-06-13).
  - Measure B would add an estimated $71 per year to the tax bill on a $325,000 assessed home
    ([county tax rate impact
    statement](https://elections.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/2026/measures/b-impact), retrieved
    2026-06-13).
- **Uncertainty:** The bond does not fund the Eastside Elementary consolidation question,
  which the district has deferred twice. Ellison does not have a position on consolidation and
  says so.
- **The strongest objection, and the answer:** *$71 a year is real money for fixed-income
  households in a county where the median age is 44.* True, and the state's senior exemption
  covers about 3,100 Ashfield households — but not renters, who pay it through rent and
  cannot claim it.
- **Conviction:** settled
- **Approved:** yes — 2026-07-28
