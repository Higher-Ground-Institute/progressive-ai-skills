## Ranked issues

### 1. Housing permit backlog

- **What it is:** The county has approved far more housing than gets built. A residential
  building permit that state law targets at 45 days is taking 287, and the permitting office
  has the same staffing it had in 2014.
- **Salience evidence:** Median residential permit turnaround was 287 days in Q1 2026, against
  a 45-day statutory target and a 2019 actual of 45 days ([Development Services Quarterly
  Report Q1-2026](https://devservices.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/reports/2026-q1), retrieved
  2026-06-10). 41 residents signed up for public comment on the Millbrook Commons delay at the
  March 12 Board meeting ([minutes](https://ashfieldcounty.example.gov/minutes/2026-03-12),
  retrieved 2026-06-10). The Millbrook Ledger has covered individual stalled projects four
  times since January.
- **Who is affected, and how many:** 2,890 approved-but-unbuilt units, plus every applicant in
  the current queue (317 open applications as of Q1-2026, same report).
- **Stage:** recurring; next decision point is the FY2027 budget markup in April 2027
- **Decision-maker:** Board of Commissioners, budget authority over Development Services
- **Who currently speaks on it:** Nobody, in any depth. The county publishes the turnaround
  figure in a PDF appendix without commentary. The Ledger has covered four individual project
  delays without ever printing the county-wide number. No commissioner has raised it on the
  record.
- **Quality of what exists:** Poor. There is no public explanation anywhere of why the backlog
  exists. The raw number is public and buried.
- **Primary records:** FY2026 adopted budget p. 143 (reviewer headcount); Development Services
  Q1-2026 report; 2023 Development Services workload study
  ([link](https://devservices.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/reports/2023-workload))

### 2. Northgate data center water usage

- **What it is:** A 340,000 sq ft data center proposed for the former Ashfield Fairgrounds,
  projecting 1.1 million gallons per day of cooling water in a county whose water authority
  declared a Stage 1 shortage in each of the last two summers.
- **Salience evidence:** Application AC-2026-0331 filed 2026-03-31 with the Planning
  Commission, projecting 1.1 MGD cooling water
  ([application](https://planning.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/applications/AC-2026-0331),
  retrieved 2026-06-14). The Ashfield Regional Water Authority declared Stage 1 shortage
  conditions in July 2024 and August 2025
  ([ARWA board minutes 2025-08-19](https://water.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/board/2025-08-19),
  retrieved 2026-06-12). Current county-wide average daily withdrawal is 8.4 MGD (same source),
  so the project would represent roughly a 13% increase.
- **Who is affected, and how many:** All 31,000 ARWA connections
- **Stage:** pending; public comment closes 2026-09-15, Planning Commission hearing not yet
  scheduled
- **Decision-maker:** Planning Commission first, Board of Commissioners on appeal
- **Who currently speaks on it:** No official, outlet, or organization has published anything
  on the water figure. The Ledger ran one 400-word story on the filing (2026-04-02) that did
  not mention water. Northgate Partners' own project page does not state a water figure.
- **Quality of what exists:** None. The 1.1 MGD figure is on page 46 of a 212-page PDF and has
  not appeared in print anywhere.
- **Primary records:** Application AC-2026-0331 pp. 44–51; ARWA board minutes 2024-07-16 and
  2025-08-19; ARWA 2025 system report

### 3. Millbrook school facilities and Measure B

- **What it is:** A $94 million school facilities bond on the November 3 ballot, driven by a
  2023 condition assessment that found four of eleven school roofs past service life.
- **Salience evidence:** Measure B certified to the ballot 2026-05-08
  ([county elections](https://elections.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/2026/measures), retrieved
  2026-06-10). The 2023 facilities condition assessment rated 4 of 11 roofs "past service life"
  with a combined $31 million replacement estimate
  ([assessment](https://millbrookschools.example.org/facilities/2023-assessment) p. 12,
  retrieved 2026-06-13). District capital reserve was $1.4 million at the FY2026 audit.
- **Who is affected, and how many:** 6,200 students; every property taxpayer in the district
  (est. $71/yr on a $325,000 assessed home)
- **Stage:** pending, decided 2026-11-03
- **Decision-maker:** voters
- **Who currently speaks on it:** The Yes on B committee publishes advocacy material. An
  opposition committee publishes a one-page flyer. The Ledger has run three stories.
- **Quality of what exists:** Mediocre and partisan on both sides. Nobody has published the
  condition assessment in readable form; it exists only as a 180-page PDF.
- **Primary records:** 2023 facilities condition assessment; FY2026 district audit; county tax
  rate impact statement

### 4. Water and sewer rate increase

- **What it is:** ARWA raised the residential base rate 22% effective 2026-01-01, the third
  increase in four years.
- **Salience evidence:** Base rate went from $38.40 to $46.85 per month effective 2026-01-01
  ([ARWA Resolution 2025-11](https://water.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/board/res-2025-11),
  retrieved 2026-06-12). Cumulative increase since 2022 is 61%.
- **Who is affected, and how many:** 31,000 connections
- **Stage:** decided; next rate case scheduled 2027
- **Decision-maker:** ARWA board, appointed by the Board of Commissioners
- **Who currently speaks on it:** ARWA publishes the resolutions. The Ledger covered the vote
  once. No elected official has explained why rates rose 61% in four years.
- **Quality of what exists:** Thin. The resolutions state the new rate but not the driver.
- **Primary records:** ARWA Resolutions 2022-04, 2023-09, 2025-11; ARWA 2025 system report

### 5. County road maintenance backlog

- **What it is:** 118 lane-miles rated "poor" in the 2025 pavement condition survey, up from
  71 in 2021.
- **Salience evidence:** [2025 pavement condition
  survey](https://publicworks.ashfieldcounty.example.gov/pci-2025), retrieved 2026-06-11.
- **Who is affected, and how many:** county-wide
- **Stage:** recurring
- **Decision-maker:** Board of Commissioners, via the annual paving program
- **Who currently speaks on it:** The public works director publishes a genuinely good
  quarterly explainer with a map and a prioritization methodology.
- **Quality of what exists:** Good. This one is already well covered.
- **Primary records:** 2025 pavement condition survey; FY2026 budget paving line
