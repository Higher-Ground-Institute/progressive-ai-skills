## Biography

Maya Ellison, 43, has lived in Millbrook since 2011 and in Ashfield County since childhood —
she grew up in Calder County and moved across the line for her husband's job. Married to
Daniel Ellison, a diesel mechanic at a truck shop on Route 9. Two children, one at Millbrook
Elementary and one at Millbrook Middle.

She worked for Ashfield County as a building inspector from 2013 to 2024 and left in March
2024 to care for her father through his last illness; he died in November 2024. Since then
she has run a one-person pre-purchase home inspection business, Ellison Inspection Services,
working roughly twenty hours a week.

## Earned standing

**Housing permit backlog** — Eleven years inspecting residential projects in this county,
2013–2024, including plan review support during two staffing gaps. She can describe the
bottleneck step by step, by desk, and she has read the file on most of the stalled projects
people complain about. What she can say that nobody else in this race can: which specific
review step the file dies at, and why it started dying in 2014. Quote from the interview:
*"That's not a zoning problem, that's a staffing problem. Four reviewers in 2014, four
reviewers now, and the permit volume nearly tripled."*

**Millbrook school facilities** — She sat on the 2023 facilities advisory committee that
produced the condition assessment. She has read all 180 pages of it and her two children
attend two of the buildings in it. What she can say that nobody else can: what is actually in
the assessment, in plain language, including the parts the Yes committee leaves out.

**The Northgate site itself** — She inspected the former Ashfield Fairgrounds site in 2019 and
again in 2022, for two separate structures. She knows the site's soil, its drainage, and its
existing utility connections. **Note: standing on the site is not standing on the water
question, and she does not have a position on the water question.** See `## No position yet`.

**County road maintenance — no standing found.** She has strong opinions and drives the roads.
So does everyone. She has never worked in public works, never sat on a transportation
committee, and cannot describe how the paving priority list is built. Recorded here because
she raised roads three times and wants it in the campaign.

**Sheriff's department budget — no standing found.** Same finding. She has never worked in or
alongside the department and has no record on it. Her own account of who covers it, from the
follow-up call: *"The sheriff puts a budget dashboard up every month, the Ledger does a story
on it every budget season, and the county's budget-in-brief has the whole thing on one page.
It's all public, people just don't read it."*

## Positions held

### Housing permit backlog

- **Position:** Fund three additional plan reviewers and a permit tracking system in the
  FY2027 budget, and publish turnaround times monthly. Concretely: move an amendment at the
  Board's FY2027 budget markup in April 2027 adding $412,000 to Development Services — three
  reviewer positions at $118,000 fully loaded plus a $58,000 tracking system — offset from the
  $2.1 million unallocated general fund balance
  ([FY2026 adopted budget](https://ashfieldcounty.example.gov/budget/fy2026), p. 143 and p.
  12, retrieved 2026-06-09).
- **Conviction:** settled
- **Reasoning, in their words:** *"Two hundred and eighty-seven days. A permit that state law
  says takes forty-five is taking two hundred and eighty-seven, and it is not because the
  rules changed. It's because there are four people doing the work of nine. I sat next to
  those four people."*
- **Uncertainty she named herself:** whether three is the right number. *"The 2023 workload
  study said somewhere between two and five and nobody has run it again since. I'd run it in
  the first quarter."*
- **The objection she expects, and her answer:** *"Somebody's going to say adding staff to a
  slow office just gets you slow staff. Fair. That's why the number gets published every
  month. If it doesn't move in two budget cycles, it's a process problem and the county should
  say so out loud."*
- **Would they say this at a hostile town hall?** yes
- **Source:** interview, 2026-06-09

### Millbrook school bond (Measure B)

- **Position:** Support Measure B. A county commissioner does not vote on the bond, so what she
  would actually do is endorse it publicly and support the county's share of the joint-use
  field agreement that depends on it.
- **Conviction:** settled
- **Reasoning, in their words:** *"Four of eleven roofs are past service life, that's thirty-one
  million dollars of roof, and the district has one point four million in the capital reserve.
  You can be against the bond, but then you have to say which four buildings you're closing.
  I've read the file on that one."*
- **What she says about the cost:** *"Seventy-one dollars a year on a three-hundred-and-
  twenty-five-thousand-dollar house is real money if you're on a fixed income. The senior
  exemption covers about thirty-one hundred households here. It doesn't cover renters, and
  renters pay it anyway through the rent."*
- **Would they say this at a hostile town hall?** yes
- **Source:** interview, 2026-06-09; figures confirmed against the 2023 facilities condition
  assessment and the FY2026 district audit, 2026-06-13

  attacked as a former insider settling scores, and expects a records request on her own
  inspection history. Her answer: *"Every file I signed is public. Go read them."*

  traffic study is released; she will not use the phrase "working families."
